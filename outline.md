# Prompt Engineering for Business Professionals

© Elephant Scale — July 26, 2026

**Duration:** 2 Days (16 hours)
**Level:** Introductory to Intermediate. Hands-on, exercise-driven. No coding required.
**Currency:** Built on today's frontier assistants — Claude (Anthropic), ChatGPT (OpenAI), and Gemini (Google) — as of 2026, including reasoning models and tool-connected assistants.

## Description

Every knowledge worker now has a general-purpose AI assistant one browser tab away, and the difference between a mediocre result and a genuinely useful one is almost entirely in how the request is written. Prompt engineering — the craft of instructing an AI clearly, giving it the right context, and refining its output — has quietly become one of the highest-leverage business skills of the decade. A well-designed prompt turns a generic chatbot into a marketing analyst, a customer-service drafting partner, a policy summarizer, or a research assistant that saves hours a week.

This two-day, hands-on course teaches business professionals to write prompts that consistently produce accurate, on-brand, useful results — and to build a reusable prompt system their whole team can rely on. It begins with how modern AI assistants actually behave (and where they fail), then works through the anatomy of a strong prompt, prompting for real business scenarios across marketing, sales, customer service, and operations, and the advanced techniques — reasoning, multi-step prompt chains, giving the model your own reference material — that separate casual users from power users. Participants also learn to test and evaluate prompts so quality is measurable, to defend against failure modes like hallucination and prompt injection, and to package winning prompts into a shared, versioned library.

The course is current to the 2026 model landscape rather than the early ChatGPT era: participants learn today's reasoning models, tool-connected assistants, and structured-output techniques, and they work against the assistants their organization is most likely to have deployed. Every concept is reinforced with a hands-on exercise, and all prompt templates, evaluation rubrics, and worked examples are provided in a shared repository so participants leave with a toolkit, not just notes. The two days close with a capstone in which each participant designs and demonstrates an AI solution for a real challenge from their own work.

### Why this course

- **Current, not 2023.** Frontier reasoning models, tool-connected assistants, and structured output — not chain-of-thought tricks that newer models have made obsolete.
- **Measurable quality.** Participants learn to *evaluate* prompts against rubrics, so "good" is defined and repeatable rather than a matter of taste.
- **Built for real work.** Every technique is applied to marketing, sales, customer service, HR, and operations scenarios — with brand voice, tone, and personalization treated as first-class concerns.
- **Safety and judgment included.** Hallucination, prompt injection, privacy, and "when *not* to use AI" are taught as core skills, not an afterthought.
- **You leave with a toolkit.** Reusable prompt templates, a starter prompt library, and evaluation rubrics — packaged to share across a team.

## Audience

Business professionals, product managers, marketing and sales specialists, customer-service and support staff, analysts, and operations and HR professionals who want to get reliably better results from AI assistants. No technical or programming background is required; anyone comfortable using a web browser and a word processor can succeed.

## Prerequisites

- Comfort using a web browser and everyday office software (typing, copy/paste, working with documents)
- A general awareness of AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is helpful but not required
- No programming, data-science, or prior prompt-engineering experience needed

## Objectives

By the end of the course, participants will be able to:

- Explain how modern AI assistants work at a practical level — tokens, context, temperature, and why they sometimes get things wrong
- Write clear, well-structured prompts using role, task, context, constraints, format, and examples
- Adapt prompts to specific business scenarios and maintain a consistent brand voice
- Apply advanced techniques — reasoning models, multi-step prompt chains, and supplying reference material — to complex tasks
- Test, compare, and evaluate prompts against quality criteria, and troubleshoot failures
- Recognize and defend against hallucination, prompt injection, and privacy risks, and judge when not to use AI
- Build, document, version, and share a reusable prompt library for team-wide adoption

## Delivery Model

- 2 training days, 8 hours per day
- Lecture, live demonstration, and hands-on exercises split roughly 50/50
- Modules build from AI fundamentals through applied and advanced prompting to evaluation, governance, and a capstone
- All exercises are self-contained and can be re-run after class

## Lab Setup

- Browser-based access to a current AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini) — the specific assistant can be matched to the client's environment
- No software installation and no coding required; all work is done in a browser
- Prompt templates, evaluation rubrics, and worked examples provided in a shared repository

## Course Outline

---

# Module 1 — How Modern AI Assistants Actually Work

**Suggested duration:** 2 hours

**Learning outcomes:**

- Describe what a large language model does and why prompting matters
- Explain tokens, context windows, temperature, and hallucination in plain language
- Compare today's leading assistants and choose the right one for a task

- The 2026 assistant landscape: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini — strengths and differences
- How language models generate text, and why the same question can give different answers
- Key terms without the jargon: models, tokens, context window, temperature, hallucination
- Reasoning models vs. fast models — when each is the right tool
- Why the prompt is the steering wheel: small wording changes, large output changes
- Common myths and misconceptions that lead to poor results

**Hands-on Exercise:** Run the same business request three different ways and compare how phrasing, context, and model choice change the answer.

**Module review:** Identify where an AI assistant adds value in your role — and where it does not.

---

# Module 2 — The Anatomy of a Strong Prompt

**Suggested duration:** 3 hours

**Learning outcomes:**

- Construct prompts using a repeatable structure rather than trial and error
- Supply context and examples that steer the model toward the desired output
- Control output format so results are ready to use

- The building blocks: role, task, context, constraints, output format, examples
- Being specific: turning a vague ask into an unambiguous instruction
- Giving the model reference material to work from instead of relying on its memory
- Zero-shot vs. few-shot: teaching by example
- Controlling tone, length, audience, and structure
- Formatting output as lists, tables, emails, or summaries you can paste straight into work

**Hands-on Exercise:** Rewrite a set of weak prompts into strong ones and measure the improvement in the results.

**Module review:** Build a personal prompt template you can reuse for everyday tasks.

---

# Module 3 — Prompting for Real Business Scenarios

**Suggested duration:** 3 hours

**Learning outcomes:**

- Apply prompt techniques to marketing, sales, customer-service, and operations tasks
- Maintain a consistent brand voice and tone across AI-generated content
- Personalize AI output for different audiences and channels

- Marketing and content: drafts, variations, repurposing, campaign ideation
- Sales and customer service: outreach, response drafting, objection handling, tone matching
- Operations and HR: summarizing, drafting policy and documentation, meeting notes, analysis
- Encoding brand voice and style so output sounds like your organization
- Personalization and segmentation: tailoring the same message to different audiences
- Working with your own data: summarizing reports, extracting key points, comparing documents

**Hands-on Exercise:** Produce a small, on-brand content set (e.g., an email, a summary, and a customer response) for a scenario from your own work.

**Module review:** Map three recurring tasks in your role to prompt patterns that handle them.

---

# Module 4 — Advanced Prompt Engineering Techniques

**Suggested duration:** 3 hours

**Learning outcomes:**

- Use reasoning models and step-by-step approaches for complex problems
- Break large tasks into multi-step prompt chains
- Supply external knowledge to ground AI output in facts

- Reasoning models: letting the AI think through multi-step problems
- Prompt chaining: breaking a big task into a sequence of smaller, reliable steps
- Grounding answers in your own documents and data (the idea behind retrieval/RAG, no code)
- System prompts and persistent instructions for consistent behavior
- Self-review and refinement: having the AI critique and improve its own output
- Tool-connected assistants: what it means when AI can search, browse, or use files

**Hands-on Exercise:** Design a multi-step prompt chain that takes a task from raw input to a finished, reviewed deliverable.

**Module review:** Decide which of your tasks justify advanced techniques and which do not.

---

# Module 5 — Refining, Testing & Evaluating Prompts

**Suggested duration:** 2 hours

**Learning outcomes:**

- Improve prompts iteratively using a deliberate process
- Define what "good" looks like and evaluate output against it
- Diagnose and fix common prompt failures

- The refinement loop: draft, test, diagnose, improve
- Defining success: accuracy, completeness, tone, structure, and trustworthiness
- Lightweight evaluation: rubrics and side-by-side comparison of prompt versions
- Spotting inaccuracies and knowing when to trust — or verify — an answer
- Failure modes: ambiguity, hallucination, and prompt injection
- Troubleshooting: turning a disappointing result into a reliable one

**Hands-on Exercise:** Evaluate two competing prompts against a rubric and troubleshoot a deliberately broken prompt.

**Module review:** Establish a personal quality bar for AI output in your work.

---

# Module 6 — Building & Governing Prompt Systems

**Suggested duration:** 1.5 hours

**Learning outcomes:**

- Package effective prompts into reusable, shareable assets
- Version and maintain prompts as they evolve
- Apply responsible-use practices and recognize when not to use AI

- Building a reusable prompt library: templates, categories, and documentation
- Versioning, quality checks, and maintenance as prompts and models change
- Sharing prompts across a team: SOPs and consistent adoption
- Responsible use: privacy, confidentiality, bias, and human oversight
- Guardrails and when *not* to use AI — the tasks that need a human
- Keeping current as models improve

**Hands-on Exercise:** Document, version, and package a reusable prompt others could adopt.

**Module review:** Draft the first entries of a prompt library for your team.

---

# Module 7 — Capstone Project

**Suggested duration:** 1.5 hours

**Learning outcomes:**

- Apply the full workflow to a real business challenge
- Present and justify an AI-assisted solution

- Choose a genuine challenge or opportunity from your own work
- Design the prompts, chain, and evaluation approach end to end
- Build, test, and refine the solution
- Present the solution and discuss trade-offs, risks, and next steps

**Hands-on Exercise:** Build and demonstrate a working AI solution for your chosen business challenge.

**Module review:** Leave with a working solution and a 30-day plan to apply prompt engineering in your role.
