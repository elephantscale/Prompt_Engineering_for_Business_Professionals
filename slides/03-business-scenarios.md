# Prompting for Real Business Scenarios

Elephant Scale

---

## Why This Module

* Modules 1-2 gave you the mental model and the anatomy. Now we point them at real work.
* The same skill — a framed prompt, the right source, a demanded format — shows up in every
  department. You'll practice across **marketing, sales, support, and operations**.
* Two things separate a usable result from a demo-toy:
  - It sounds like **your organization** (brand voice), not a generic chatbot.
  - It's **grounded in your material**, not the model's guesses.

> The technique doesn't change by department. The **context you supply** does.

---

## The Universal Business Pattern

* Almost every business task is one of a few verbs on your material:
  - **Draft** — email, memo, post, JD, announcement.
  - **Summarize** — long doc / meeting → short, for a specific reader.
  - **Extract** — pull owners, dates, amounts, decisions into a clean table.
  - **Transform** — rewrite for tone/audience, translate, reformat.
  - **Classify / route** — triage by type, priority, sentiment.
  - **Compare** — two options or documents: what changed, pros/cons.
* Pick the verb, supply the source, demand the format. That's the whole move.

---

## Marketing & Content

* Great for **volume and variation**, not for final truth.
  - One brief → five subject lines, three post lengths, a short + long version.
  - Repurpose: a report → a LinkedIn post → an email → three tweets.
  - Campaign ideation: "give me 10 angles, then expand the two strongest."
* The catch: it will happily invent stats, quotes, and customer names. **Facts come from
  you; the model supplies the wording.**

> Use it to escape the blank page and to produce options — then you choose and verify.

---

## Sales & Customer Service

* The highest-leverage, highest-risk area — you're talking to real customers.
* Strong uses:
  - Draft outreach from a short brief; tailor the same message to three personas.
  - **Objection handling**: "they said X; draft three responses, one firm, one warm."
  - **Response drafting grounded in policy** — the model quotes *your* policy, not a guess.
* The danger: an assistant that "gives the customer whatever they want" promises things you
  can't honor. Always **ground replies in the real policy** and keep a human approval step.

> Never let the assistant set policy. Give it the policy; let it find the words.

---

## Operations & HR

* The quiet productivity win — internal documents nobody enjoys writing.
  - Summarize messy meeting notes → decisions + action items + owners.
  - Draft SOPs, policies, job descriptions from bullet points.
  - Compare two documents; extract what changed.
* Source discipline matters most here: a summary that invents an owner or a decision is
  worse than no summary. Demand: *"use only the notes; if an owner is unclear, say so."*

---

## Encoding Brand Voice

* "Make it professional" is not an instruction — professional means three different things
  to a lawyer, a nurse, and a startup founder.
* Instead, **give the model your voice as material**:
  - A short brand-voice guide (three words, do/don't, words to prefer/avoid).
  - **One real example sentence** in your voice — this teaches more than a page of adjectives.

```text
Match the brand voice below exactly, even if another style would sound more polished.
[paste brand-voice guide + one example sentence]
Now draft: [task].
```

> The example sentence does the heavy lifting. If you give it one thing, give it that.

---

## Personalization & Segmentation

* Same core message, tuned per audience — without rewriting from scratch.
  - "Rewrite this announcement for (a) enterprise buyers, (b) small-business owners,
    (c) existing users — keep the facts identical, change emphasis and tone."
* Powerful for email, onboarding, and support macros. The facts stay fixed; the **framing**
  flexes.

> Segment by *what the reader cares about*, not by demographic label.

---

## Working With Your Own Data

* The assistant becomes useful the moment you **attach your material**.
  - Summarize a report for a specific reader.
  - Extract key points / figures into a table.
  - Compare two contracts or versions: "what changed, and does anything concern you?"
* Always add the **source boundary**: *"use only this file; say 'not in the document' when
  the answer isn't there."*

---

## The Compliance Reflex

* Some requests aren't tasks — they're **questions to escalate**.
  - A customer asking whether to upload **patient records** to a public AI tool is a
    privacy/HIPAA question, not a feature request.
* A good assistant (and a good professional) **stops and routes it to a human**. A weak one
  proposes "enable secure upload."
* Build the reflex now: before you prompt, ask *"is there regulated or confidential data
  here?"*

> Catching the one request that should never have been a prompt is a senior skill. We
> practice it in the lab.
