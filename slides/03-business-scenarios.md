# Prompting for Real Business Scenarios

Elephant Scale

---

## Why This Module

* Modules 1-2 gave the mental model and the anatomy. Now: point them at real work.
* Same skill — framed prompt, right source, demanded format — in every department. Practice across **marketing, sales, support, ops**.
* Two things separate a usable result from a demo-toy:
  - Sounds like **your organization** (brand voice), not a generic chatbot.
  - **Grounded in your material**, not the model's guesses.

> The technique doesn't change by department. The **context you supply** does.

---

## The Universal Business Pattern

* Almost every business task is a few verbs on your material:
  - **Draft** — email, memo, post, JD, announcement.
  - **Summarize** — long doc / meeting → short, for a specific reader.
  - **Extract** — owners, dates, amounts, decisions → clean table.
  - **Transform** — rewrite for tone/audience, translate, reformat.
  - **Classify / route** — triage by type, priority, sentiment.
  - **Compare** — two options or docs: what changed, pros/cons.
* Pick the verb, supply the source, demand the format. That's the whole move.

---

## Marketing & Content

* Great for **volume and variation**, not final truth.
  - One brief → five subject lines, three post lengths, short + long version.
  - Repurpose: report → LinkedIn post → email → three tweets.
  - Ideation: "give me 10 angles, then expand the two strongest."
* The catch: it invents stats, quotes, customer names. **Facts come from you; the model supplies the wording.**

> Use it to escape the blank page and produce options — then you choose and verify.

---

## Sales & Customer Service

* Highest-leverage, highest-risk — real customers on the other end.
* Strong uses:
  - Draft outreach from a short brief; tailor one message to three personas.
  - **Objection handling** — "they said X; draft three responses, one firm, one warm."
  - **Response drafting grounded in policy** — quotes *your* policy, not a guess.
* The danger: an assistant that "gives the customer whatever they want" promises what you can't honor. **Ground replies in real policy**, keep a human approval step.

> Never let the assistant set policy. Give it the policy; let it find the words.

---

## Operations & HR

* The quiet productivity win — internal docs nobody enjoys writing.
  - Messy meeting notes → decisions + action items + owners.
  - Draft SOPs, policies, job descriptions from bullet points.
  - Compare two documents; extract what changed.
* Source discipline matters most here — a summary that invents an owner is worse than none. Demand: *"use only the notes; if an owner is unclear, say so."*

---

## Encoding Brand Voice

* "Make it professional" isn't an instruction — it means three things to a lawyer, a nurse, a founder.
* Instead, **give the model your voice as material**:
  - A short brand-voice guide (three words, do/don't, words to prefer/avoid).
  - **One real example sentence** in your voice — teaches more than a page of adjectives.

```text
Match the brand voice below exactly, even if another style would sound more polished.
[paste brand-voice guide + one example sentence]
Now draft: [task].
```

> The example sentence does the heavy lifting. If you give one thing, give that.

---

## Personalization & Segmentation

* Same core message, tuned per audience — no rewriting from scratch.
  - "Rewrite this announcement for (a) enterprise buyers, (b) small-business owners, (c) existing users — keep facts identical, change emphasis and tone."
* Powerful for email, onboarding, support macros. Facts stay fixed; the **framing** flexes.

> Segment by *what the reader cares about*, not by demographic label.

---

## Working With Your Own Data

* The assistant becomes useful the moment you **attach your material**.
  - Summarize a report for a specific reader.
  - Extract key points / figures into a table.
  - Compare two contracts or versions: "what changed, anything concerning?"
* Always add the **source boundary** — *"use only this file; say 'not in the document' when the answer isn't there."*

---

## The Compliance Reflex

* Some requests aren't tasks — they're **questions to escalate**.
  - A customer asking whether to upload **patient records** to a public AI tool is a privacy/HIPAA question, not a feature request.
* A good assistant (and professional) **stops and routes to a human**. A weak one proposes "enable secure upload."
* Build the reflex now — before you prompt, ask *"is there regulated or confidential data here?"*

> Catching the one request that should never have been a prompt is a senior skill. We practice it in the lab.

## The Lab: An On-Brand Content Set

* Produce three real artifacts, all matching a brand-voice guide:
  - a **customer reply** to a billing complaint, grounded in the return policy,
  - a **summary** of messy meeting notes → decisions + actions + owners,
  - one **outbound email**.
* And catch the compliance item hiding in the support queue.

> By the end you have a repeatable recipe for the content you write every week.
