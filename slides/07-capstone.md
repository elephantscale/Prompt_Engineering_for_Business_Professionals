# Capstone Project

Elephant Scale

---

## Why This Module

* Where the course stops being exercises and becomes **your work**.
* Take a real, recurring challenge from your own job. Build a solution you could use Monday.
* Everything from two days comes together:
  - The prompt **anatomy** (Module 2)
  - A business scenario in your **brand voice** (Module 3)
  - A **chain / grounding / reasoning** step, *only where it earns its place* (Module 4)
  - A **scored evaluation** against the rubric (Module 5)
  - Packaged as a **reusable, documented** library entry (Module 6)

> The test isn't "it's clever." It's "someone else on my team could run this and get the same result."

---

## What You Will Deliver

* A **working prompt or prompt chain** that solves a real task.
* A **before / after** — manual vs. AI-assisted, gain named in real numbers.
* At least one **deliberate guardrail** you can explain (source boundary, review step, privacy rule).
* A **library entry** (the Module 6 template) so the prompt is reusable.
* A **30-day adoption plan** — laptop to team.
* A short **demo** — two to three minutes — showing it run.

> Six small pieces, not a research project. You have the tools; this is assembly.

---

## Choosing a Good Challenge

* Pick something **real, recurring, and worth it**:
  - **Real** — a task you actually do, not a hypothetical.
  - **Recurring** — weekly or daily beats once-a-year; reuse is the payoff.
  - **Worth it** — enough time or friction that a better way matters.
* Good candidates: weekly status summaries, support-reply drafting, meeting notes → action items, first-draft JDs, competitive-research digests, RFP answers.
* **Avoid**: confidential/regulated data in a public tool, one-off tasks, "replace my judgment entirely."

> Can't name who benefits and how often? Pick a different task.

---

## The Build Loop

* You know this loop — now run it end to end on your own task:
  1. **Frame** with the anatomy (role, task, context, constraints, format).
  2. **Ground** in your real source material (attach it; "use only this").
  3. **Chain** *only if* one prompt can't do it reliably — extract → draft → review.
  4. **Evaluate** against the rubric. Score it; don't eyeball it.
  5. **Refine** — change one thing at a time, re-score.
  6. **Package** the winner as a library entry, saved as a Project / Custom GPT / Gem.

> Don't reach for a chain or reasoning model to look sophisticated. Use the simplest thing that clears the bar.

---

## Build It in Your Assistant

* Do this in **your own account** — Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini.
* Save the final version where it's reusable:
  - **Claude → a Project** — source material and instructions attached.
  - **ChatGPT → a Custom GPT** — role and files baked in.
  - **Gemini → a Gem** — standing instructions.
* That saved, named assistant *is* your deliverable running — not a prompt you retype.

> A prompt you paste from a doc each time is a draft. A saved Project/GPT/Gem is a product.

---

## Guardrails Are Part of the Grade

* Every capstone shows at least one guardrail chosen **on purpose**:
  - **Source boundary** — "use only the attached material; if it's not there, say so."
  - **Human review step** — what a person checks before anything ships.
  - **Privacy rule** — what data is *not* allowed near this tool.
  - **Uncertainty flag** — "mark anything unverified as *needs review*."
* Name the one failure you're most worried about, and the control that catches it.

> "Where could this be confidently wrong, and who catches it?" — answer that out loud.

---

## The 30-Day Plan

* A prompt only you can run, only today, isn't a win. Plan the handoff:
  - **Week 1** — use it yourself daily; log where it breaks.
  - **Week 2** — refine from the log; lock the version; write the library entry.
  - **Week 3** — hand to one colleague; watch them run it cold; fix what confused them.
  - **Week 4** — share to the team; set a re-test reminder for the next model update.

> The prompt is the easy part. Adoption is the work. Plan for it.

---

## Presenting Your Solution

* Two to three minutes. Structure:
  1. **The problem** — and how often it happens.
  2. **The before** — how long / how painful manually.
  3. **The solution** — run it live or show the saved output.
  4. **The guardrail** — the risk you designed against.
  5. **The plan** — your next 30 days.
* Be honest about limits. "Here's where it still needs me" is a *strength*.

> Scored against the capstone rubric — business fit, prompt quality, technique fit, output quality, safety & judgment, rollout. Aim for all six, not a perfect one.

---

## What Good Looks Like

* Solves a task the presenter **actually has**; payoff named in numbers.
* A prompt someone else could run and get the **same** result.
* Advanced technique used **only where it earned its place**.
* A **scored** before/after, not "it felt faster."
* A real **guardrail** and a real **review step** that stays.
* A concrete **30-day plan**, not "I'll use it sometimes."

> You leave with a working solution and a plan to apply it — the whole point of two days.
